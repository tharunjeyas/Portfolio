01 // Problem Statement

Automotive crash structures
need to do more.

Traditional crash box geometries suffer from stress concentration and poor deformation control under high-velocity impacts. This research explores how bio-inspired and mathematically-derived geometries can offer fundamentally better energy absorption behaviour.

02 // Methodology

Three geometries.
One rigorous test.

01
CAD Modelling
3D parametric models created for all three crash box geometries — Traditional, Honeycomb, and Voronoi — maintaining equivalent overall dimensions.
02
Explicit Dynamics
ANSYS Explicit Dynamics solver used to simulate high-velocity impact at 30 m/s using aluminium alloy material properties throughout.
03
Comparative Analysis
Deformation, stress distribution, and internal energy absorption tracked over simulation time to rank crashworthiness performance.

Key Finding

The irregular geometry of Voronoi tessellation distributes impact loads across a stochastic cell network — preventing localised failure modes that plague uniform geometric patterns.

"Voronoi structures provide the most efficient energy absorption due to their irregular geometry, enabling continuous deformation and minimising stress concentration."
05 // Conclusion

Voronoi wins.
Here's why it matters.

Performance Summary

The Voronoi crash box demonstrated superior crashworthiness across all measured parameters — highest internal energy absorption, most uniform stress distribution, and well-controlled progressive deformation throughout the 6.7 ms impact event.

Design Implications

Bio-inspired stochastic geometries offer a compelling alternative to traditional and periodic structures in automotive safety applications. Future work may explore topology optimisation of Voronoi seed placement and density gradients to further tune crash response.
